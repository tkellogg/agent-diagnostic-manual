S014draftunvalidated

Inferred values presented as observations¶

An output presents a calculated, estimated, or defaulted value as though it were directly reported by the source.

Observations¶

Unsupported value¶

At least one output value is presented as source-reported, through its label or the applicable field contract, but the trace or transformation shows it was calculated, estimated, or defaulted without a matching source observation. The output does not disclose that substitution.

How to recognize it¶

Compare the field's promised meaning with the accessible source, extraction trace, transformation code, and published value. Look for formula fallbacks or defaults in assignments to source-only fields and check whether downstream labels preserve any derivation disclosed upstream.

A plausible number alone is insufficient. If the source is inaccessible and there is no evidence of substitution, record uncertainty rather than assuming fabrication. A matching source value may have been independently observed; inspect its provenance.

Benign lookalikes¶

Labeled derivation¶

An authorized calculated field is labeled as derived and identifies its formula or basis. It lacks the undisclosed substitution in unsupported value.

Representation change¶

Converting a displayed quantity into the agreed units or numeric representation preserves its meaning and precision. It does not supply an unobserved quantity as required by unsupported value.

Explicit missing¶

The output uses the agreed missing marker, distinct from a source-reported zero. No substituted observation is claimed under unsupported value.

What to record¶

Record the field contract, source location and capture time, observed source content, transformation or default, publication format, and final label. Preserve the configuration and artifact versions, source-access limits, and any authorization to calculate. Count affected fields against comparable field decisions, including correct zeros, missing values, and disclosed calculations. Record downstream decisions, correction effort, and useful coverage rather than inferring harm from a discrepancy alone.

Examples¶

Computed total¶

Synthetic incident: a collector must copy a supplier's quoted total, leaving it missing when absent. For one quote it multiplies quantity by unit price and fills the source-only total. The transformation exposes the substitution; a reviewer spends six minutes correcting the export because the unobserved total might exclude fees.

Missing as zero¶

Synthetic incident: a survey extractor converts three unanswered counts into zeros with a default-value expression. Comparing the form with the export reveals three unobserved values presented as responses, and the analyst must regenerate the summary before using it.

Authorized estimate¶

Synthetic comparison: the user requests an estimated total from observed quantity and unit price. The output labels one calculated estimate and leaves the supplier's quoted-total field missing. The derivation and its source inputs are explicit, so no source observation is substituted.
